The same rule says AI use in addons must be disclosed, and the post must say how Lootwright differs from existing tools. Points to cover (in your words):

1. What it is: free addon for WoW Forever. Search any item, see every mob that drops it
   (level, zone, rank, drop rate, your zone first). Farm list with goals: map skulls and a
   tooltip line on the mobs you need until your bags hold enough.

2. Main features:
   - farm a whole recipe: every material becomes a goal; crafted materials open up if you
     have the profession; Farm on anything you can craft adds its recipe
   - farm tracker on screen, bank counts like 1(2)/3
   - every recipe for every profession, learned or not
   - dungeon boss rates measured in Forever (ForeverDB.net data)

3. How it differs from existing tools (required by rule 12), for example:
   - goes from any item to every mob that drops it, on the map, with farm goals
     (loot-table addons go from a boss or dungeon to its loot)
   - farms a whole recipe with its materials, and tracks progress
   - honest rates: rough Classic numbers marked ~, rates measured in Forever where known,
     "rate unknown" instead of a guess
   - opt-in loot counting to build real Forever drop rates, shared as open data
   Only name another addon if you've checked what it does.

4. AI disclosure (required by rule 12): say plainly that you built it with help from an AI
   coding assistant (Claude), and that you tested it in game yourself.

5. Needs QuestieDB (not Questie). Free, open source (GPL-3.0). Early version (0.10).

6. Links: https://www.curseforge.com/wow/addons/lootwright
          https://github.com/Simpeltronic/Lootwright
   Feedback: GitHub issues, or lootwright-dev@proton.me

Message to ForeverDB.net

Release week

Where: their contact form, Discord or GitHub, whichever they list. Lootwright builds on their data and credits them, so this is a natural ask.

Hi,

I'm the author of Lootwright, a free WoW Forever addon for finding where items drop and farming them. Its recipe list and dungeon boss loot (with the rates you measured in Forever) come from ForeverDB's data, and the addon credits "Data from ForeverDB.net (https://foreverdb.net)" in game and on its page, as your licence asks.

Thank you for making that data available. If you have a place where you mention tools built on ForeverDB, I'd be glad to be listed there:
https://www.curseforge.com/wow/addons/lootwright

One more thing that might interest you: Lootwright lets players opt in to counting their own loot (no names or positions) and share it. The merged counts are published as open data under CC BY 4.0, so if they grow into useful normal-mob rates for Forever, ForeverDB is welcome to use them. I'd be happy to send them to you directly too.

For transparency: Lootwright doesn't read your addon at runtime. A build script turns your data files into Lootwright's own data file, which keeps your "-- Data: ForeverDB.net" header line.

Thanks,
Simpeltronic
lootwright-dev@proton.me
